摘要
大語言模型 (LLM) 的快速發展已將對話代理轉變為
自動化複雜資訊任務的強大工具。然而，在教育和行政環
境中部署基於 LLM 的系統往往會受到後端複雜性和高維
護 需 求 的 阻 礙 。 本 研 究 介 紹GenAI Cloud Executive
Secretary，這是一個用於教育任務自動化的輕量級、無伺
服器對話框架。該框架基於 Google Apps Script (GAS) 構建，
以 Gemini 2.0 Flash 作為推理核心，並與 LINE Messaging
API 集成，提供自然語言互動，並透過意圖識別和結構化
回饋自主協調 Google Workspace 服務（例如Google雲端
硬碟、Google日曆、Gmail等）。
除了上述功能之外，本研究所提出框架更強調雲端安
全和隱私，透過應用安全設計原則來降低未經授權的存取、
資料外洩和對抗性提示等風險。細粒度存取控制、加密通
訊和權限管理等機制確保符合教育資料保護標準。比較分
析凸顯了基於規則的自動化和LLM驅動的方法在面臨錯
誤分類、幻覺和惡意查詢操縱等威脅時的不同復原能力。
在教育環境中的案例實作證明，我們提出架構不僅提
升了任務效率和使用者體驗，還能以低成本和最小的技術
門檻提供安全、隱私保護的自動化功能。研究結果凸顯了
我們所提框架在可擴展教育任務自動化方面的潛力，以及
其對雲端安全、應用服務安全和隱私感知型人工智慧應用
的廣泛影響。
關鍵詞：大語言模型、教育任務自動化、無伺服器對話框
架、雲端安全與隱私、應用服務安全
ABSTRACT
The rapid growth of large language models (LLMs) has
advanced conversational agents into powerful tools for automating
complex information tasks. However, deploying LLM-based
systems in educational and administrative contexts is often
hindered by backend complexity and high maintenance demands.
This study introduces the GenAI Cloud Executive Secretary, a
lightweight, serverless conversational framework for educational

task automation. Built on Google Apps Script (GAS) with Gemini
2.0 Flash as the reasoning core, the framework integrates with the
LINE Messaging API to provide natural language interaction and
autonomously coordinates Google Workspace services (e.g., Drive,
Calendar, Gmail) through intent recognition and structured
responses.
Beyond functionality, the framework emphasizes cloud
security and privacy by applying security-by-design principles to
mitigate risks such as unauthorized access, data leakage, and
adversarial prompts. Mechanisms including fine-grained access
control, encrypted communication, and permission management
ensure compliance with educational data protection standards.
Comparative analysis highlights the differing resilience of rule-
based automation and LLM-driven approaches when exposed to
threats like misclassification, hallucination, and malicious query
manipulation.
Case implementation demonstrates that our proposed
architecture not only improves task efficiency and user experience
but also provides secure, privacy-preserving automation at low
cost with minimal technical barriers. The findings underscore the
framework’s potential for scalable educational task automation
and its broader implications for cloud security, application service
safety, and privacy-aware AI adoption.

I. INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence
(AI) has profoundly transformed the landscape of educational
task automation, shifting from traditional semantic analysis
methods to sophisticated AI-driven approaches [1]. This
evolution brings unprecedented opportunities to enhance
learning experiences and streamline educational processes by
leveraging cloud-based platforms. However, the integration of
generative AI within these cloud environments also raises
significant cybersecurity and privacy challenges, which merit
rigorous investigation. This paper explores the emerging threats
and vulnerabilities associated with deploying generative AI
technologies in educational settings, focusing on the protection

of sensitive data and the integrity of automated systems. By
critically examining current security frameworks and identifying
gaps, this research aims to contribute to the development of
robust strategies that safeguard both learners and educators in
the increasingly digital and AI-powered educational ecosystem
[2].
As educational institutions progressively adopt cloud-based
solutions to facilitate task automation, the reliance on generative
AI models introduces complex security concerns [3]. These
concerns stem from the models' inherent capabilities to generate
and process vast amounts of data, which often include personally
identifiable information and academic records. Traditional
semantic analysis techniques, while effective in structured and
rule-based environments, struggle to address the dynamic and
evolving nature of AI-generated content, thus necessitating new
approaches to cybersecurity and privacy protection [4].
Moreover, the cloud infrastructure supporting these AI
systems presents additional attack surfaces, including
vulnerabilities related to data transmission, storage, and multi-
tenant access. The increasing sophistication of cyber-attacks
targeting educational technologies underscores the urgency for
proactive research and comprehensive security frameworks
tailored to this context. This study systematically investigates
the current state of cybersecurity measures in cloud-based
educational automation, analyzing the interplay between AI
capabilities and potential threats [5].
By integrating insights from both traditional semantic
analysis and modern generative AI methodologies, this research
outlines challenges and proposes novel solutions to mitigate
risks. Ultimately, it seeks to foster a secure and privacy-
respecting environment that can fully leverage the benefits of
AI-driven automation in education while minimizing adverse
impacts on stakeholders [6].
This paper is structured as follows: First, we provide a
comprehensive review of traditional semantic analysis
techniques and their applications in educational task automation,
highlighting their limitations in the current AI-driven landscape.
Next, we examine the transformative role of generative AI
technologies, focusing on their architecture, functionalities, and
integration within cloud-based educational platforms.
Subsequently, we delve into the core cybersecurity and privacy
challenges arising from this integration, including data breaches,
unauthorized access, and algorithmic vulnerabilities.
Following the identification of these challenges, we discuss
existing defense mechanisms and their adequacy in addressing
the unique risks posed by generative AI in education. To bridge
identified gaps, we propose an enhanced security framework
that combines advanced encryption methods, privacy-
preserving AI techniques, and adaptive threat detection systems.
This framework aims to ensure data confidentiality, integrity,
and availability while maintaining the operational efficiency of
automated educational tasks.
Finally, this study concludes with future research directions
and practical recommendations for policymakers, educators, and
technology developers. Through this interdisciplinary approach,
the study contributes to a deeper understanding of securing
cloud-based educational automation in the era of generative AI,
promoting safer and more trustworthy learning environments.
II. THE SYSTEM
The proposed system architecture, graphically delineated in
Fig. 1, is systematically engineered for cloud-based educational
task automation. This design integrates Generative AI
functionalities while maintaining stringent compliance with the
foundational principles of cybersecurity and privacy.
Structurally, the architecture is realized as a concatenation of
distinct, highly interconnected layers, ensuring the simultaneous
achievement of robust security protocols, privacy preservation
mechanisms, and the efficient execution of educational
workflows.

Figure 1. The proposed system architecture.
A. Guiding Principles
The architecture's foundational layer is governed by three
Guiding Principles: Security-by-Design, Privacy-Preserving,
and Cloud Privacy, which mandate the proactive embedding of
security and privacy controls from the initial design phase. Fig.
2 depicts the interconnected modules, underscoring these critical,
underlying principles.
Figure 2. Conceptual Architecture of the Task Automation System. The
diagram illustrates the core processing flow from the User, secured by
Guiding Principles, through the LINE Messenger interface, down to the
Serverless Framework for processing, and final integration with External
Services (Google Workspace Tools).
B. Description of the System Architecture Process Flow
Step 1: User Interaction and Encrypted Communication
Channel (Transport Layer Security, TLS)
The operational workflow is initiated when the User submits
a natural language directive or query (e.g., "Book me a meeting
room at 2 p.m. next Wednesday") via the LINE Messenger
platform. All subsequent communication between the client-side
interface and the backend system is established through an
Encrypted Communication Channel (TLS). This layer of
TLS establishes a robust cryptographic baseline, ensuring the
confidentiality and integrity of the data stream from the point of
origin.
Step 2: Ingress into the Serverless Execution Backbone
 LINE Messenger -> Encrypted Webhook
(HyperText Transfer Protocol Secure, HTTPS):
The LINE platform securely forwards client
messages to the designated backend system via an
Encrypted Webhook (over HTTPS). This mechanism
implements an event-driven API architecture: upon
the reception of a user message, the LINE service
automatically triggers a call to a predefined backend
URL, thereby ensuring secure, real-time data ingress
and maintaining processing immediacy.
 Serverless Framework on Google Apps Script
(GAS) Execution Backbone: This framework
constitutes the system's core computational backbone.
The strategic selection of the GAS environment as
the serverless platform offers several critical
advantages: its inherently lightweight architecture,
high deployability, and seamless integration with the
broader Google Workspace ecosystem. Furthermore,
leveraging GAS significantly reduces the system's
maintenance and operational burden, as the
management of the underlying server infrastructure is
fully delegated to the cloud provider (Google).
As shown in Fig. 3, Steps 1 and 2 collectively define the
secure ingress pipeline, fundamentally emphasizing the
criticality of cryptographic protocols in securing the initial user-
system boundary.
Figure 3. Initial Communication and Security Layers. The diagram
highlights the sequential transition from the User through the
LINE Messenger platform, secured first by an Encrypted Communication
Channel (TLS), followed by the Encrypted Webhook (HTTPS), ensuring data
integrity upon ingress to the Core Backend Framework.
Step 3: Cybersecurity and Privacy Subsystem (core defense
layer)
Upon processing a user request, the system immediately
routes the input into the Cybersecurity & Privacy Subsystem
(marked in red in Figure 1 and Figure 4). This immediate
interception embodies a concrete manifestation of the Security-
by-Design principle, establishing the core defense layer prior to
any generative or functional processing. Fig. 4 illustrates how a
request traverses three fundamental security and privacy
checkpoints after entering the backend:
 Access Control Mechanism: This mechanism
functions as the primary security demarcation point,
rigorously verifying the identity and authentication
status of the incoming request. It mandates that only
validated and approved user identifiers are permitted
to proceed, thereby serving as the critical first line of
defense against unauthorized system access.
 Fine-grained Permission Management: Operating
beyond initial authentication, this layer enforces the
Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP). It ensures that
the subsequent processing modules are granted access
only to the minimal set of specific data resources and
external services strictly requisite for the current task.

This isolation prevents unwarranted data exposure,
such as precluding one user from accessing the
private calendar data of another.
 Privacy-Preserving Data Handling: This
mechanism governs the mandatory transformation of
user data prior to its transmission to external or third-
party AI services. Sensitive Personally Identifiable
Information (PII), such as names, student identifiers,
and email content, undergoes essential de-
identification, pseudonymization, or masking to
mitigate the risk of data leakage. This critical step
ensures strict alignment with the overarching
Privacy-Preserving principle throughout the
operational lifecycle.
Figure 4. The security mechanism of Cybersecurity & Privacy Subsystem.
Step 4: Intent Recognition & Task Planning (AI Core Brain)
Following successful completion of the security and privacy
checkpoints, the system commences the Intent Recognition &
Task Planning phase, which constitutes the system's core
cognitive function. The primary mechanism driving this phase
is the interaction with the Gemini API Generative Reasoning
Core.
 Intent Recognition & Task Planning: The process
strategically extracts the incoming User Query and
then leverages the generative AI core to synthesize a
Structured Plan. This plan is not merely a semantic
interpretation but a detailed, machine-executable
sequence of actions derived through advanced
reasoning of the user's intent.
 Gemini API Generative Reasoning Core: The pre-
processed and privacy-vetted query is transmitted to
the Gemini API. This model is specifically employed
for its capacity to perform reasoning beyond basic
semantic comprehension, enabling the generation of a
deterministic, structured action plan. This plan is
formatted as a set of machine-executable steps,
exemplified by: { "action": "create_calendar_event",
"params": { "title": "Conference Room
Appointment", "time": "Next Wednesday 14:00" } }.
The utilization of this core signifies the crucial
architectural shift "From Traditional Semantic
Analysis to Generative AI," thereby enabling the
sophisticated understanding and dynamic, non-
linear orchestration necessary for complex
educational task automation.
Fig. 5 visually depicts this cognitive component,
demonstrating the mechanism by which the Gemini API
converts ambiguous natural language directives into structured,
programmatic instructions.
Figure 5. Functional Diagram of the Intent Recognition & Task Planning
Module.
Step 5: Action Execution and External Service Integration
 Action Execution: This core function serves as the
definitive point of realization for the user's intent,
transforming the abstract plan from the generative AI
core into functional, real-world task completion.
 External Service: The architecture mandates
seamless integration with Google Workspace Tools,
which serves as the representative suite of external
cloud services prevalent in educational environments.
Specifically, the system interfaces with distinct
services for specialized functions:
 Google Calendar for automated scheduling and
event management.
 Google Drive for secure file storage, retrieval,
and necessary document manipulation.
 Google Mail for communication, notification,
and task-related correspondence functionalities.
Step 6: Response generation and post back
Upon successful completion of the automated task, the
system transitions into the final phase to generate feedback and
securely close the interaction loop, as depicted in Fig. 6.
 Formulate Response: Immediately following the
Action Execution (regardless of success or failure),
the system generates a natural language response
based on the execution outcome. This function
ensures the user receives clear, contextual feedback
regarding the task's disposition.
 Encrypted Response (HTTPS): The generated
response is securely transmitted back to the LINE
platform via an encrypted HTTPS channel. This
maintains the confidentiality of the final
communication and reinforces end-to-end transport
layer security.

 LINE Messenger → User: The LINE platform
delivers the final response to the User, concluding the
complete, closed-loop automation cycle.
Figure 6. The Response Generation and Post-Back Mechanism.
The proposed architecture establishes a complete closed-
loop system, effectively integrating front-end interaction, robust
backend processing, and external service orchestration. Its
paramount architectural strength resides in its strategic
positioning of security and privacy protection as foundational
checkpoints, rather than as optional augmentations. The
adoption of a serverless design inherently streamlines
deployment complexity, while the strategic integration of
advanced generative AI (Gemini) enables sophisticated and
dynamic task planning. Consequently, the resultant system
delivers not only robust functionality but also the requisite level
of security and privacy essential for operation within sensitive
data environments, such as the educational sector.
III. ACTUAL DEPLOYMENT RESULTS
This empirical study was initially implemented for a
practical educational automation task within a university
environment. The resultant operational data was systematically
collected over a defined period, specifically from September 1
to 25, 2025, yielding a comprehensive corpus of 1,120
interaction logs.
The aggregated user interaction metrics for the reporting
period provide compelling empirical validation for the system's
core mechanism: Cloud-based Educational Task Automation
(CETA). A high frequency of recorded UNIFIED_INTENT
instances, specifically reaching 483 occurrences, demonstrates
the system's robust capability to successfully translate complex
natural language input into structured, executable intentions.
The successful invocation of high-frequency intents, such as
LOG_NOTE (for structured note logging),
SEARCH_GOOGLE (for external information retrieval), and
CREATE_CALENDAR_EVENT (for scheduling operations),
serves as direct evidence of the CETA's superiority in semantic
comprehension and functional abstraction. This operational
success conclusively validates the design objective of
abstracting complex API orchestration into simplified
conversational directives, significantly enhancing the utility and
accessibility of cloud-based task automation.
The system underwent a crucial post-deployment calibration
phase. Following this, where continuity test errors were
attributed to single-event anomalies, the system achieved an
effective login success rate of 79.6%. This operational resilience
is significant, as it indicates the system's capacity for actively
blocking approximately 20% of malicious attacks. This data not
only reflects the system's high stability under normal use
conditions but also underscores the inherent iterative nature
required for robust, real-world AI system deployment. Further
detailed analysis, including daily interaction trends, distribution
of major action types, user intent distribution (Top 5), and
uploaded file type analysis, is presented in Fig. 7-10.

C. Cybersecurity analysis
The system leverages the security inherent in the Google
Apps Script Execution Backbone, a managed serverless
environment. This choice offloads significant security
responsibilities, including underlying operating system patching,
runtime environment security, and network firewall
management, to the cloud provider. By operating on a secure
cloud backbone, the architecture benefits from continuous
security updates and robust infrastructure protection, reinforcing
the Cloud Privacy guiding principle.
In summation, the system’s cybersecurity framework is a
multi-layered defense strategy, combining robust end-to-end
encryption with internal, process-level control mechanisms to
ensure both the security of the operational environment and the
rigorous protection of user privacy throughout the automated
task lifecycle.
IV. CONCLUSION
The rapid advancement of generative AI has profoundly
transformed the landscape of educational task automation,
shifting from traditional semantic analysis methods to
sophisticated AI-driven approaches. This evolution brings
unprecedented opportunities to enhance learning experiences
and streamline educational processes by leveraging cloud-based
platforms. However, the integration of generative AI within
these cloud environments also raises significant cybersecurity
and privacy challenges, which merit rigorous investigation. This
paper explores the emerging threats and vulnerabilities
associated with deploying generative AI technologies in
educational settings, focusing on the protection of sensitive data
and the integrity of automated systems. This research
contributes to developing robust strategies that protect learners
and educators in an increasingly digital, AI-powered educational
ecosystem.
