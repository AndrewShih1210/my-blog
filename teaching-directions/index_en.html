Yu-Ting Shih (Andrew Shih)

Adjunct lecturer at National Taichung University of Education and doctoral candidate in Educational Information and Measurement.

These are directions for discussion, not a schedule of active courses. Plans depend on age, prior knowledge and local conditions.

Choose a starting point by learning stage

Elementary school

Begin with observation, description, stories and fact-checking through teacher demonstrations and paper-based activities.

Junior high school

Modify templates and work in groups to practice workflow design, testing and explaining decisions.

Senior high school

Integrate data inquiry and interactive projects, retaining sources, tests and revision records.

University

Connect disciplinary questions with interdisciplinary projects, adapting technical depth to prior experience.

Graduate study

Focus on literature, research workflows and result verification, with researchers responsible for methods and academic integrity.

Adult learning

Start with work or everyday tasks, adjusting practice to digital experience, equipment and purpose.

Choose a practical application

People often have several needs. Start with one recurring task rather than trying to learn every feature.

Administration

Forms, notifications, task tracking and routine operations: understand the workflow before automating.

Documents and data

Task briefs, summaries, report outlines and spreadsheets, with checks against source material.

Multimedia and communication

Visual stories, scripts, video and subtitles, from communication goals to permissions and review.

Management and collaboration

Task decomposition, workflow handoffs and review responsibilities, including where AI should not make decisions.

Applied computing and automation

Web tools, Google Apps Script (GAS), Google Sheets and LINE Bot, with attention to data flows and limitations.

Teaching, research and assessment

Activities, learning prompts, research data and assessment criteria, emphasizing evidence, suitability and human judgment.

Learning through understanding, practice and review

Plain language, gradual progression

Explain concepts through familiar situations before using tools. Beginner-friendly learning still requires practice.

Create and explain

Keep requirements, tests and revisions so learners can explain what they did and why they changed it.

Build responsibility into practice

Practice source verification, privacy, permissions and usage limits; do not treat AI output as automatically correct.
